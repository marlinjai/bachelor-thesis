Labour economics · applied micro

AI and labour markets

The most crowded area on this list, and still the richest. The trick is to find an outcome nobody has measured yet rather than re-running an exposure regression.

Is AI hollowing out the bottom rung of the career ladder?

Test whether AI-exposed occupations cut junior postings faster than senior ones — the mechanism everyone asserts and few have measured. Map job postings to O*NET task-exposure scores in the Felten and Eloundou tradition, then run a difference-in-differences across occupations around late 2022.

Data: Lightcast or Indeed Hiring Lab posting microdata, O*NET, BLS OES.

Does AI talk on earnings calls predict anything real?

Count AI mentions in transcripts, then look two to three years forward at revenue per employee, margins or measured TFP. A null result is publishable here: it would say the disclosure is cheap talk, which is itself the finding.

Data: Refinitiv or Seeking Alpha transcripts, Compustat, Capital IQ.

Does AI compress wages inside the firm?

The experimental literature finds the largest gains at the bottom of the skill distribution. If that holds at scale, within-firm wage dispersion should be falling in adopting firms. This is the highest-value question here and the hardest to execute.

Data: IAB LIAB or the Danish employer–employee registers — apply early, lead times run to months.

International trade · policy evaluation

Trade fragmentation and tariffs

Tariff regimes create sharp, dated, well-documented shocks. The data is free, the institutions are legible, and supervisors know how to grade this work.

How much tariff evasion runs through third countries?

Compare what the United States records importing from a country against what that country records exporting to the United States. The gap, cross-checked against surging re-exports from Vietnam and Mexico, measures transshipment. A classic mirror-statistics design, and unusually current.

Data: UN Comtrade mirror statistics, US Census trade data, national customs releases.

Who actually pays the tariff?

Trace pass-through from border prices into shelf prices by product category, and ask where along the chain the incidence lands. The interesting variation is cross-category: brands with pricing power absorb, commodities pass through.

Data: BLS import/export price indices, CPI microdata, Census import values by HS code.

Reshoring announcements versus reshoring capex

Announcements are free; factories are not. Match reshoring press releases to subsequent property, plant and equipment in filings, and measure the conversion rate. The gap between the two is the paper.

Data: Reshoring Initiative announcements, company press releases, Compustat PP&E.

Environmental economics · natural experiment

Carbon policy as a natural experiment

Two live European policy changes with dates you did not choose — which is exactly what makes them worth using.

What did CBAM's definitive regime do to European sourcing?

The carbon border adjustment moved from reporting-only to actual certificate obligations at the start of 2026, covering steel, cement, aluminium, fertiliser, hydrogen and electricity. Compare covered CN codes against near-neighbour codes that fall just outside scope, and measure import reallocation, price effects, or the pass-through onto non-EU exporters.

Data: Eurostat Comext at CN-8 level, EU ETS price series, national CBAM registry disclosures.

Public data DesignDiD on CN-code scope Feasibility

Greenhushing after the sustainability-reporting rollback

The Omnibus simplification package cut the set of firms inside CSRD scope. Firms that fell out are a natural control-versus-treated split: did their disclosure volume drop, did the tone change, and did anything happen to their cost of capital? Tests whether the reporting was ever informative.

Data: Sustainability reports (scraped), ESG rating providers, bond spreads or Refinitiv equity data.

Energy economics · spatial

Energy, data centres and the grid

The fastest-moving cost shock in the developed world right now, and the academic literature has barely started. Everything here runs on free grid and county data.

Do data centres raise local electricity prices?

Match hyperscaler siting announcements to wholesale price zones and run an event study on locational marginal prices, then check whether the effect reaches retail tariffs. The question is politically charged and empirically open — a good combination for a thesis with an audience beyond your committee.

Data: PJM Data Miner, ERCOT, or ENTSO-E Transparency; utility interconnection queues; company siting announcements.

What does a data centre actually do for the local economy?

Large construction bump, small permanent payroll, generous tax abatement. Use synthetic control on treated counties to price out whether the subsidies clear, and look at house prices and rents alongside employment.

Data: BLS QCEW county employment, county assessor records or Zillow ZHVI, state abatement filings.

Electricity prices and industrial location in Europe

Do energy-intensive plants follow electricity prices across European borders after 2022? Be explicit about the confounders — the German fiscal expansion and defence procurement move the same regions at the same time, and handling that honestly is half the thesis.

Data: Eurostat industrial electricity prices, PRODCOM output, Orbis or national plant registers.

Empirical finance

Finance

Two frontier topics, one safe replication. If you need certainty that you will have results by the deadline, the last entry is the insurance policy.

Does private credit remove bank risk or just relocate it?

Banks have replaced direct corporate lending with lending to the funds doing the direct corporate lending. Trace the exposure end to end and ask whether the consolidated risk actually left the banking system, or simply acquired an extra layer.

Data: Fed H.8 releases, FR Y-9C call reports, BDC filings, LSTA/DealScan for the syndicated comparison.

Public data DesignExposure mapping & panel Feasibility

Are prediction markets better forecasts than the experts?

Score Kalshi and Polymarket prices against polls and analyst consensus on the same resolved events. Calibration curves, the favourite–longshot bias, and cross-venue arbitrage on identical contracts each make a chapter. Free API data and a scope you can dial up or down at will.

Data: Kalshi and Polymarket public APIs, FiveThirtyEight-style poll archives, IBES consensus for the financial contracts.

Stablecoin reserves and the front end of the curve

Reserve requirements under the new US regime push issuers into Treasury bills. Size the demand, then test whether issuance growth moves bill yields or the bill–OIS spread. A clean bridge between crypto and money markets that avoids the usual crypto-thesis pitfalls.

Data: Issuer reserve attestations, TreasuryDirect auction results, FRED bill yields, on-chain supply series.

Passive ownership and price informativeness in Europe

Well-trodden ground in the US, thinner in Europe. Not the most original thesis on this list, and it is the one least likely to fail: the method is settled, the data is standard, and a competent replication with European institutional detail is a defensible contribution.

Data: WRDS, FactSet ownership, Compustat Global.

Industrial organisation

Competition and pricing

Where the regulators are actively looking for evidence, which means a good thesis here has readers outside the university.

Do pricing algorithms collude without being told to?

Germany's Markttransparenzstelle für Kraftstoffe publishes every price change at every filling station in the country, to the minute. Date the adoption of algorithmic repricing by station or chain and test for supracompetitive coordination afterwards — margins, price-cycle synchronisation, response asymmetry.

Data: MTS-K price history (free, registration required), Tankerkönig archives, station ownership records.

Personalised pricing and consumer welfare

The behavioural route: run a vignette or incentivised experiment on willingness to accept personalised prices, and whether disclosure changes it. Choose this if you would rather design your own data than fight for someone else's.

Data: Self-collected via Prolific or a university subject pool; pre-register the design.

Did banning drip pricing lower what people pay?

Junk-fee rules arrived at different times in different jurisdictions, which gives you staggered adoption. Scrape headline and checkout prices for hotels, tickets or deliveries across the boundary and measure whether total prices fell or only their presentation changed.

Data: Scraped listing and checkout prices, regulatory effective dates by jurisdiction.

Marketing science · applied micro

Consumer behaviour and industry disruption

Three shocks working through consumer-facing industries right now, each with a treatment intensity you can actually measure.

What do weight-loss drugs do to the grocery basket?

Regional prescription penetration gives you a continuous treatment; scanner data gives you category-level outcomes. Snacks, alcohol and confectionery against staples is the obvious cut, and the substitution pattern within categories is the more interesting one.

Data: NielsenIQ or Circana scanner panels, IQVIA or state-level prescription statistics.

Zero-click search and the economics of publishing

AI answer panels rolled out on a staggered schedule by market and query type. Match that timing to referral traffic and advertising revenue for publishers, and ask what happens to the supply of the content the answers are built on.

Data: Similarweb or Semrush traffic, publisher filings and press disclosures, rollout dates by market.

What return-to-office mandates cost the firms that issue them

Mandates are dated, public and firm-specific — a ready-made event study. Look at the announcement return, then at senior attrition in the following year, and ask whether the people who leave are the ones the firm could least afford to lose.

Data: Announcement dates from press coverage, LinkedIn or Revelio Labs profiles, CRSP returns.

Macroeconomics · public finance

Fiscal policy and public economics

Europe's fiscal stance changed more in the last two years than in the preceding fifteen. Regional exposure gives you identification that pure time-series macro cannot.

Multipliers from Germany's infrastructure and defence turn

Build a Bartik instrument from pre-existing regional exposure to defence contractors and construction, then estimate employment and output responses at Kreis level. The identifying assumption is that pre-period industry shares are unrelated to the shock — defend it carefully and you have a real macro thesis.

Data: Destatis regional accounts, Bundesagentur für Arbeit employment, federal procurement awards.

Public data DesignBartik / shift-share Feasibility

Does defence spending crowd out civilian research?

As NATO members raise spending toward the new targets, something in the budget gives. Use a cross-country panel to ask what: civilian R&D, public investment, or transfers. Small N is the obvious weakness, so lean on within-country reallocation rather than cross-country levels.

Data: SIPRI military expenditure, OECD government budget appropriations for R&D, IMF GFS.

Public data DesignCross-country panel Feasibility

Does upzoning actually lower rents?

Auckland, Minneapolis and the recent European reforms give you dated, discrete supply-side interventions and untreated comparison cities. Synthetic control on rents and permits. The method is settled, the data is free, and the policy question is genuinely contested.

Data: Municipal permit records, Zillow or national rent indices, OECD housing prices.

How to choose

Theses fail on logistics far more often than on ideas. Before you commit to anything above, run it through these three tests — a topic that fails any one of them will cost you a term.

Data today

You can download the core dataset this week, not data you hope to negotiate access to. An application in progress is not a dataset.

A treatment with a date

Something changed, on a specific day, for reasons unrelated to your outcome. Without that you are writing correlations.

A control group in one sentence

If you cannot name the comparison group without hedging, your identification is not ready and your defence will find the gap.

To turn any of these into a full proposal, I need three things from you

  • Your programme — finance, management, or economics. It changes which of these a committee will accept.
  • Your econometrics comfort level, honestly. Staggered DiD and synthetic control are not the same lift as a pass-through regression.
  • Whether your university has WRDS, NielsenIQ or research-data-centre access. This alone eliminates about a third of the list.
