\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

\section{Interpreting the Estimates}
\label{sec:interpretation}

To be written once \Cref{ch:results} is filled. The interpretation should be
disciplined by two things stated in advance.

First, the sign and size expected under the theory. \textcite{branger2014} and
\textcite{bohringer2012} imply a measurable but modest volume response
concentrated in the most emissions-intensive covered codes. A coefficient far
larger than that is more likely to indicate a contaminated control
(\Cref{sec:substitution}) than an unusually strong policy.

Second, the possibility of a null. Given \textcite{martin2016} and
\textcite{naegele2019}, a null is a live outcome and would be a finding rather
than a failure --- particularly because the definitive regime is the first
setting in which free allocation is being withdrawn rather than shielding the
result. A null here says something the earlier \acrshort{ets} nulls could not.

\section{Mechanisms}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

Three mechanisms could generate a negative volume estimate, and customs data
distinguishes them only partially:

\begin{description}
  \item[Genuine reallocation] European buyers switch to lower-carbon origins or
    to domestic supply. Predicts a fall in high-intensity origin shares
    \emph{and} a rise in low-intensity shares.
  \item[\Glsentrytext{resourceshuffling}] Exporters redirect their cleanest
    existing output to the EU without changing production. Predicts a fall in
    \emph{reported} intensity with no change in origin shares --- and requires
    origin-level production data to confirm, which this thesis does not have.
  \item[Downstream shifting] Buyers move to uncovered finished goods.
    Predicts a fall in covered codes with a matching rise downstream.
\end{description}

The honest position is that this design can rule the third in or out
descriptively, can distinguish the first from the second only under an
assumption about origin production being fixed in the short run, and should say
so rather than presenting reallocation as established.

\section{Policy Implications}
\label{sec:policy}

The pass-through result from RQ3 is the one with direct policy content. If
\acrshort{cbam} is largely absorbed by non-EU exporters, it functions as a
terms-of-trade transfer towards the Union and the political economy of
extending it is straightforward. If it is largely passed through, it is a cost
on European downstream manufacturers, and the case for extending scope
downstream --- rather than merely widening it across inputs --- becomes much
stronger.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Short post-period.} Two to three quarters. Nothing here speaks
    to long-run supply-chain reorganisation.
  \item \textbf{Relative, not absolute.} The estimate is relative to
    near-neighbour products. Economy-wide anticipation effects are differenced
    out, so the estimate is a lower bound on the total effect.
  \item \textbf{No firm-level data.} Comext is product--origin--month. Which
    importers adjusted, and whether adjustment concentrated among large
    declarants, is unobservable here.
  \item \textbf{Emissions intensity is imputed.} $e_{io}$ in \Cref{eq:dose}
    comes from sectoral benchmarks, not from verified installation data.
    Measurement error in the dose attenuates the intensity estimates.
  \item \textbf{One jurisdiction.} Whether the result generalises to other
    border adjustments is untested and should not be asserted.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Further Work}
\label{sec:further-work}

The design is deliberately re-runnable. As later phases extend Annex~I to
further \acrshort{cn} codes, each extension supplies a new treatment date with
a control group constructed by the same rule --- turning a single natural
experiment into a staggered one, at which point the
\textcite{callaway2021} machinery becomes necessary rather than optional.
Merging the registry's verified emissions declarations once published would
also replace the imputed $e_{io}$ with observed values and allow the
resource-shuffling question to be settled rather than bounded.
