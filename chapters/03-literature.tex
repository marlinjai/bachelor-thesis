\chapter{Related Literature}
\label{ch:literature}

Three strands bear on the question: the theory of \gls{carbonleakage} and its
correction, the ex-post evidence on the \acrshort{ets}, and the econometrics of
\acrshort{did} with staggered or dose-varying treatment. The first two set the
prior; the third determines what can credibly be estimated.

\section{Leakage and Border Adjustment in Theory}
\label{sec:lit-theory}

Simulation studies of unilateral carbon pricing consistently predict leakage,
though they disagree about how much. The Energy Modeling Forum's coordinated
comparison across twelve models \parencite{bohringer2012} finds leakage rates
clustering between five and twenty percent, and border adjustment cutting them
by roughly half. \textcite{branger2014} meta-analyse a wider set of studies and
report that border adjustment restores most of the competitiveness loss in
covered sectors while doing considerably less for aggregate emissions --- a
distinction that matters for how the results of this thesis should be read.
\textcite{fischer2012} compare border adjustment against output-based rebates
and show the two are close substitutes in welfare terms but differ sharply in
who bears the cost, which is the theoretical counterpart of RQ3.

\textcite{fowlie2018} is the necessary corrective to all of this. They argue
that leakage risk as operationalised in policy --- typically trade intensity
multiplied by emissions intensity --- is a poor proxy for the elasticity that
actually matters, and that the sectors flagged as at-risk are not reliably the
ones that would relocate. Since \acrshort{cbam}'s scope was drawn using
essentially that operationalisation, their critique implies the covered set may
not be where the largest effects are.

\section{Ex-post Evidence on the EU ETS}
\label{sec:lit-ets}

The empirical record is notably quieter than the models. \textcite{martin2016}
survey a decade of \acrshort{ets} evaluations and conclude that the scheme
reduced emissions without detectable harm to employment, output or
competitiveness. \textcite{naegele2019} test for leakage directly in European
manufacturing and find no robust evidence of it, though they are careful to
note that free allocation makes this a test of the policy as implemented rather
than of carbon pricing as such. \textcite{dechezlepretre2017} reach a similar
verdict across environmental regulation more broadly.

\textcite{aichele2015} provide the closest methodological precedent for the
present design. Studying Kyoto commitments, they find that ratifying countries
increased the carbon content of their imports by roughly five percent and
reduced the carbon intensity of domestic production --- evidence of
composition-shifting rather than wholesale relocation. That is precisely the
pattern RQ2 is constructed to detect, and it motivates treating
\gls{resourceshuffling} as the leading alternative explanation rather than an
afterthought.

The synthesis is a prior worth stating plainly: theory expects a measurable
effect, and the ex-post literature has repeatedly failed to find one. The
definitive \acrshort{cbam} regime is the first setting in which the free-
allocation confound is being deliberately removed, which is what makes a null
result here informative rather than merely another quiet finding.

\section{Estimation}
\label{sec:lit-econometrics}

The design is a two-group, single-date \acrshort{did}, which avoids the worst
of the staggered-adoption problems catalogued by \textcite{goodmanbacon2021}
and \textcite{dechaisemartin2020}: with one treatment date there are no
already-treated units serving as controls, so the \acrshort{twfe} estimator
does not decompose into badly-weighted comparisons. Treatment intensity does
vary over time through the free-allocation phase-out
(\Cref{sec:dose}), and \textcite{callaway2021} supply the appropriate
group-time estimator should the dose structure be modelled explicitly.

For inference and pre-trends, \textcite{borusyak2024} give the efficient
event-study estimator used for the dynamic specification, and
\textcite{rambachan2023} the sensitivity analysis that replaces the usual
pre-trends test. This matters more than it may appear: a flat pre-trend plot is
weak evidence, and reporting the largest post-treatment violation of parallel
trends under which the result survives is substantially more honest.

Because trade flows are non-negative, heavily skewed and contain genuine
zeros, the level specification is estimated by \acrfull{ppml} following
\textcite{silva2006}, with the high-dimensional fixed effects handled as in
\textcite{correia2020}. \textcite{head2014} is the reference for the gravity
scaffolding. Standard errors are clustered at the \acrshort{cn}-code level;
with a moderate number of clusters the wild bootstrap of
\textcite{cameron2008} is reported alongside.
