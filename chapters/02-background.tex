\chapter{Institutional Background}
\label{ch:background}

This chapter establishes three things the empirical design depends on: what
exactly \acrshort{cbam} obliges importers to do, when each obligation took
effect, and why the boundary of its product scope can be treated as
econometrically useful rather than merely administrative.

\section{The EU ETS and the Leakage Problem}
\label{sec:ets}

The \acrfull{ets}, established by Directive 2003/87/EC
\parencite{eu2003ets}, caps emissions from power generation and
energy-intensive industry and lets installations trade allowances. Because the
cap binds only inside the Union, it raises the marginal cost of European
production relative to imports. The standard response has been
\gls{freeallocation}: sectors deemed at risk of \gls{carbonleakage} receive
allowances without charge, benchmarked on output rather than emissions.

Free allocation blunts the relocation incentive, but it also blunts the price
signal that the cap is supposed to send, and it transfers rents to incumbent
installations. \acrshort{cbam} is the Union's attempt to remove the subsidy
without reintroducing the leakage risk: the border levy substitutes for free
allocation rather than adding to it.

\section{How CBAM Works}
\label{sec:cbam-mechanics}

Under Regulation (EU) 2023/956 \parencite{eu2023cbam}, an importer of a covered
good must

\begin{enumerate}
  \item register as an authorised declarant;
  \item report the \gls{embeddedemissions} of the goods imported, using either
    verified actual emissions or prescribed default values;
  \item surrender \acrshort{cbam} certificates covering those emissions, priced
    on the \acrfull{eua} market; and
  \item deduct any carbon price already paid in the country of origin.
\end{enumerate}

Two design features matter for identification. The deduction in step~4 means
the levy is a \emph{differential} on the carbon-price gap, not on emissions as
such --- so origins with their own carbon price should respond less. And the
certificate price is tied to the \acrshort{eua} market, which means the size of
the treatment varies over time with a price the importer does not control. Both
features are exploited in \Cref{ch:strategy}.

\section{Product Scope as an Identification Boundary}
\label{sec:scope-boundary}

Coverage is enumerated by \acrshort{cn} code across six sectors: cement, iron
and steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. The relevant
property is not the list itself but its edges. Consider two eight-digit codes
that differ by a processing step, an alloy content, or a form factor, where
only one falls inside the annex. Such pairs share buyers, freight routes,
input costs and business-cycle exposure, and they differ in exactly one
respect: whether a certificate must be surrendered.

This is the source of the control group. \Cref{ch:data} sets out how candidate
pairs are selected and, importantly, how pairs are \emph{rejected} when the
uncovered code is a plausible substitute for the covered one --- because a
substitute is not a control. If importers switch from a covered code to its
uncovered neighbour, the neighbour is treated by spillover and the
\acrshort{did} estimate is inflated on both sides.

\section{What CBAM Cannot Do}
\label{sec:cbam-limits}

Three limits bound what any estimate can mean. The mechanism covers direct
embedded emissions and, for some sectors, a defined set of indirect emissions,
but it does not cover the emissions of downstream goods: a steel beam is
covered, a washing machine containing steel is not. This creates an incentive
to import further down the value chain, which the product-scope design will
register as a \emph{null} on covered codes rather than as leakage. Second,
default values are generous relative to best-available-technology benchmarks,
so exporters with clean production have an incentive to verify and dirty ones
do not --- a selection problem in the reported-emissions data, though not in
the trade data used here. Third, \gls{resourceshuffling} is not detectable
from customs records at all; it requires origin-level production data, and
\Cref{sec:mechanisms} explains how far the reallocation results can be pushed
before that constraint binds.
