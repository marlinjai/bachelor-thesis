\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\selectlanguage{english}

Reusing reclaimed timber in load-bearing structures offers considerable
potential for resource efficiency and a circular construction economy. After
decades in service, one question remains open: does the residual strength of a
used beam differ significantly between its former compression zone and its
former tension zone?

A beam under load carries compressive stress in its upper fibres and tensile
stress in its lower fibres. Over a long service life, this load history may
damage the two zones differently: micro-crack growth, creep, viscoelastic
deformation and fatigue act asymmetrically, with brittle failure dominating in
tension and ductile cell-wall buckling in compression.

This thesis reviews the physical mechanisms behind such a difference and
develops a test methodology to quantify it. Six areas were covered: German
waste-wood regulation, structural grading standards, the material physics of
load history, the state of research on reclaimed timber, the formal
requirements of Eberswalde University for Sustainable Development, and the
experimental design itself.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} reclaimed timber, reuse, strength, load history,
compression zone, tension zone, circular economy
