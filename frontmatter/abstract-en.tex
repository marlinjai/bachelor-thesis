\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\selectlanguage{english}

On 1 January 2026 the European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism moved
from a reporting-only transitional phase to a definitive regime in which
importers must surrender certificates against the embedded emissions of covered
goods. This thesis asks whether that switch measurably redirected European
sourcing.

Coverage is defined by an explicit list of Combined Nomenclature codes, so
covered products can be compared against near-neighbour codes that sit just
outside the annex --- similar goods, similar buyers, similar cyclical exposure,
but no certificate obligation --- in a difference-in-differences design on
monthly Eurostat Comext import records. Import volumes, the reallocation of
import shares across origins of differing carbon intensity, and unit values as
a proxy for price pass-through are each examined.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} carbon border adjustment, CBAM, carbon leakage,
difference-in-differences, trade reallocation, EU ETS
